Every therapy session quietly moves through three invisible stages.

Before the session can happen, someone has to make it possible.

During the session, clinicians need the right information at the right time.

After the session, providers need to document care and get paid correctly.

This wasn’t a collection of features. It was the operating model behind one therapy session.

It all begins before the therapist ever enters the room.

Before the session.

Four answers. Resolve them and the session can exist; miss one and it can’t.

At first, these decisions existed in different parts of the product.

As CarePlan evolved, we brought them together into one appointment model.

Instead of treating the appointment as a calendar event, we treated it as the source of truth for everything that followed.

Once an appointment was created, every downstream workflow already understood its context.

Freeze frame

Providers completed care inside CarePlan, then left the platform to generate invoices elsewhere.

That wasn’t just inconvenient. It exposed a gap in how we understood Australian healthcare billing.

As we worked with providers, we discovered that every session carried financial rules that affected everything downstream.

Private billingBulk billingGap fees GSTAuthorised paymentsRefunds

The invoice wasn’t the difficult part. Representing the logic behind it was.

Prototype · Billing statesLooping

Pending payment. No invoice yet. Charge patient. Paid at booking. The same session, carrying a different financial state at every point.

One of the biggest changes we made was moving payment selection into the appointment itself.

An appointment could no longer exist without a billing pathway.

Instead of billing reacting to the session, the session carried the information billing needed from the beginning.

Getting that right took multiple product and engineering iterations.

Real providers uncovered edge cases we couldn’t predict in design reviews.

Authorisation flows.
Incorrect charges.
Refunds.

Every iteration made the billing engine more faithful to how care is actually delivered.

Prototype · InvoicesLooping

Pending. Successful. Refunded. Rebill. Every one of those states came from something that happened in production.

Prototype · Medicare claimsLooping

A claim isn’t paid or unpaid. It sits in a state Medicare controls, and the product has to represent every one of them.

Once the session carried billing context… everything else became easier to coordinate.

Prototype · Notes on a patient recordLooping

Every note is dated, attributed and marked when it has been edited. A note isn’t a document you finish. It’s a record you keep adding to.

We redesigned documentation to reflect that reality.

Instead of treating notes as something that had to be completed immediately, we made them part of a living clinical record.

Providers could return to previous sessions, add observations, and build on earlier context without losing the original record.

Documentation stopped being an administrative task. It became part of continuity of care.

Individually, these workflows solved different problems. Together, they became one connected platform.

By the end of the redesign, CarePlan no longer felt like a collection of healthcare features.

Every workflow revolved around the same question:

What does this therapy session need next?

That question shaped how appointments were created, how clinicians documented care, how providers got paid and how patients returned for future sessions.

The product became simpler—not because healthcare became simpler, but because the software finally reflected the way care actually works.

Reflection

Good enterprise products don’t remove complexity. They organise it.

When I joined CarePlan, I thought I was designing healthcare software.

Looking back, I think I was designing something quieter. The invisible systems that allow one therapy session to feel effortless.

When the underlying model reflects the real world, the interface can finally disappear—and clinicians can focus on the people they’re there to help.
