Case Study 01

From inventory financing to supply chain intelligence

We started building what we thought was a tool for distributor loans. It became the foundation for how African businesses move goods.

Role

Design & Strategy Lead

Team

8 people

Status

The problem wasn't obvious from the outside

At TafiCasa, we set out to build operating systems for African businesses, starting with the supply chain space. The first thing we learned is that this space is still heavily manual. Paper documents, phone calls, fragmented data everywhere.

We narrowed our focus and spoke to the people closest to it. Banks issuing inventory-backed loans, merchants producing goods, and distributors moving them across markets.

What kept coming up

A few things were consistent across every conversation.

🗨

No single place for banks, merchants, and distributors to collaborate

📄

Pickup slips were paper based and easy to forge or duplicate

👁

Visibility across financing, inventory, and distribution was nearly zero

⚠

Decisions were reactive because no one had the full picture

30% of distributor loans in a single quarter were flagged for delayed repayment. The main reason was that there was no traceable pickup or proof of product release.

We didn't try to replace what people already knew. We designed Flux to make their existing processes work better.

We started with the banks because they're the most structured users. Highly regulated, deeply process driven. Our tools had to match how they already work without adding complexity on top.

What we built

Four core pieces that work together to give every stakeholder visibility into the same system.

.01
Shared collaboration platform

Banks, merchants, and distributors in one space. No more back channel calls or email chains to confirm what's happening.

Then we realized we were only solving half the problem

Banks loved what we built. But merchants and distributors kept telling us they needed more than a financing tool. They needed visibility into their own operations.

We stepped back and mapped every feature we'd shipped. Each person on the team wrote down what they thought Flux actually was. The words that came up most were supply chain, inventory, product movement, and visibility.

That changed the direction of the product

Before

Product receipts were financial instruments used to track product movement.

  • Built around financing workflows
  • Designed primarily for banks
  • Functioned as a system of record
→

After

Product receipts became data nodes in a larger system, enabling:

  • Operational visibility across stakeholders
  • Performance tracking for merchants
  • Decision support for the entire chain
Distributor access

Meeting distributors where they are

Not every distributor is comfortable using a dashboard. Some don't have laptops. Most are used to WhatsApp.

So we designed Flux Lite. A simplified mobile version focused entirely on pickups. Log in, request a pickup, get a code, show it at the depot. That's the whole thing.

Flux Lite mobile
Reflections

What this project changed for me

Design isn't just what it looks like. It's what gets adopted. And in this space, adoption is everything.

What started as an inventory financing tool turned into something broader. We weren't just enabling transactions. We were sitting on operational data that no one was using.

Instead of continuing to optimize workflows, I focused on how the system could become an intelligence layer. One that supports visibility, insight, and decision making across the entire supply chain.

That shift reshaped how I think about product design. The most impactful work isn't always about building more. Sometimes it's about recognizing what you've already built and seeing where it actually wants to go.
