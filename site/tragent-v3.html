The problem

Agents don’t struggle because planning is difficult.

They struggle because a single trip lives in a dozen places at once — supplier threads, WhatsApp messages, embassy checklists, documents, spreadsheets and half a dozen booking sites.

Nothing about the work is unclear. Nothing about it is in one place either.

Research

Three agents. Three different bottlenecks.

Early discovery rather than a study. Three interviews, and the brainstorming that came out of them, are where the feature list came from — more than thirty items, and the shape underneath them.

The finding that reordered everything

Visa isn’t a step in the process. It’s the gate everything else waits on.

In the first draft, visa sat in a pre-trip bucket. Agent 02 described the opposite: it’s checked first, because it decides whether the trip exists at all.

So planning runs beside it, not after it — what waits at the gate is committing money. And approval always sits with a consular officer, so the product tracks readiness and never predicts.

The product

One workspace. Every trip.

Instead of managing visas, itineraries, suppliers and documents separately, everything begins with creating a structured trip.

From the agent’s home to a structured trip. Everything that follows hangs off this object.

Agents run the system

Visas, itineraries, documents, payments, supplier coordination and the trip itself. They can also manage clients who never sign up.

Travellers experience it

Visa status, required actions, confirmations, a clear itinerary. They can upload documents and check in. They can’t control the workflow — that’s the point, not a limitation.

Scope

Three stages. V1 is the first two.

Building all three at once would have meant a shallow version of each, so V1 covers where an agent’s time actually goes and stops before the part that only matters once trips are live.

01

Pre-trip

Visa requirements and guidance, document checklists and validation, client intake and profiling, feasibility.

Built

02

Trip planning

Itinerary as the core, flights and accommodation, supplier outreach and status tracking, client and document management.

Built

03

Trip management

Live itinerary tracking, traveller check-ins, safety monitoring, incident and escalation handling.

Not built

Stage three isn’t hypothetical — Agent 02 gave me two live incidents, including a traveller detained after unknowingly crossing a border without the right visa. It needs designing after the planning system has met a real trip, not before.

Where it stands

Designed, built, and paused.

Designed and built across pre-trip and planning, but never yet in front of an agent. No adoption numbers, no impact claim.

01

Does the BCC pipeline hold at volume?

Forwarded threads, edited subject lines, suppliers replying from a different address. The unmatched flow is a hypothesis about how often that happens, not a measurement.

02

How different are agents from each other?

Three interviews, three bottlenecks. V1 serves the first two. Whether access is a later segment, a different product or the larger market is unresolved.

03

What is the upstream AI layer actually for?

Drafting outreach, chasing non-responses, flagging a stale rate. All candidates, none decided — the answer comes from watching an agent work inside V1.

04

Could AI do the understanding, not just the drafting?

Three directions explored upstream of planning: a request in the agent’s own words, an assessment of what’s missing before planning starts, a read on how ready a trip really is. None of it shipped in V1.

Looking back

The lesson wasn’t about AI. It was about sequencing.

The conversations produced a feature list. More than thirty items, and the list itself was roughly right. What I had wrong was the order.

Visa guidance sat as an early task in a linear flow. One agent describing his actual week turned it into the dependency the entire product hangs off.

Once the trip existed as one structured object, every feature knew where it belonged. Order is the part that decides whether a system fits the way someone already works.
